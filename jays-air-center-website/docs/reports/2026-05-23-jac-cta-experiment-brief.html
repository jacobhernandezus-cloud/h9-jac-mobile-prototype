Waitlist CTA Experiment

We're running a short, low-cost experiment on the jaysaircenter.com call-to-action to see which language drives more hangar inquiries. Here's what changed, what we're testing, and what happens in two weeks.

The Short Version

The "Join Waitlist" button has been quietly replaced with a three-variant test. Each visitor sees one of three labels, picked randomly and held consistent for that person. We measure who clicks and who completes the form. On June 6, we keep the winner.

What We Changed

Last week we added a privacy-respecting analytics layer to the website. It tracks the conversion funnel — visit, scroll, click, form started, form completed — without using cookies and without collecting any personal data. The data lives in our own infrastructure, not a third-party vendor.

On top of that, the single "Join Waitlist" button was replaced with a randomized three-variant test. The form behavior, your inbox, and everything downstream is unchanged.

Why Test Instead of Just Swapping the Button

"Join Waitlist" reads as passive — visitors don't actually know what they're joining or what they get in exchange. We could replace it with what we think sounds better, but the audience for a hangar at SNA — high-net-worth aircraft owners — has their own intuitions about luxury-brand language, and we'd be guessing.

Two to four weeks of real data is essentially free and tells us the actual answer instead of our best guess.

The Three Labels in Rotation

  • 01

    Join Waitlist Control

    The current label. Acts as the baseline so we can measure relative lift, not just absolute clicks.

  • 02

    Reserve Your Hangar Variant A

    Possession framing ("your"), specific to the headline service, implied scarcity. The pattern Tesla and NetJets use on their reservation pages.

  • 03

    Inquire About Availability Variant B

    Luxury-brand register — the language Aman, Ferrari, and Rolex use. Signals a dialogue rather than a transaction, which tends to lower perceived friction at the top of the funnel.

What We're Measuring

The conversion funnel, end to end:

01
Visit — visitor lands on the page
02
Engagement — scrolls past hangars / FBO / offices / tie-downs
03
CTA click — taps the call-to-action
04
Form started — clicks into a form field
05
Form completed — your inbox gets the inquiry

Roadmap

  • May 23 – May 30
    Week 1
    Baseline data collection Three variants live in equal rotation. We watch for technical issues but make no decisions. No action needed on your end — inquiries arrive in your inbox exactly as before.
  • May 30 – June 6
    Week 2
    Variants accumulate completions By the end of this week we should have enough form-completion data per variant to begin reading signal. The data set continues to grow.
  • June 6
    Decision point
    Review and decide We compare conversion rate (form completed ÷ CTA click) across the three labels and choose what to do next.

What We Decide On June 6

Three possible outcomes

  • Clear winner. If one variant beats the control by 20% or more on conversion rate, with enough completions per arm to be confident, we retire the other two and lock in the winner site-wide.
  • Too close to call. If the variants are within a few percentage points of each other, we either extend the test by two more weeks for more data, or accept that label choice isn't the bottleneck and move on to testing the next part of the funnel (e.g., the form length, the section copy, the photography).
  • Surprise insight. If a variant we expected to win underperforms, that's also a finding — it tells us something about how this specific audience reads luxury-brand language at SNA, and shapes how we write everything else.

One Thing to Know

Each visitor consistently sees the same label across visits. If you load jaysaircenter.com and see "Join Waitlist," refreshing the page won't change it — that's by design, so the experiment produces clean data. To see the other variants yourself, open the site in an incognito window or clear browser data.
